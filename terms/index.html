2. What Tally Voice does

Tally Voice connects to your QuickBooks Online company and answers questions about it. You reach it through the mobile app or through the Telegram bot, and you can type or speak.

It reads your books

Every figure it quotes is fetched from QuickBooks at the moment you ask. It can tell you:

  • your cash position, and what your day looks like in a single briefing;
  • who owes you money, and how much is outstanding;
  • your customer and supplier lists;
  • the bills you owe;
  • your products and services;
  • your profit and loss; and
  • your BAS amount and when it is due.

It also writes to your books

This is the part to read twice, because it is easy to assume an assistant only looks things up. Tally Voice can create records in your QuickBooks company. Specifically, it can create:

  • invoices, with a customer and line items;
  • customers, with the contact details you give it; and
  • products and services.

These are real records in your real books. An invoice created this way is a QuickBooks invoice like any other. Section 3 explains the step that stands between the assistant proposing one and it existing.

3. How it writes to QuickBooks

Every write goes through the same two steps, and the second one is yours. The assistant proposes. You confirm. Only then does anything reach QuickBooks.

When you ask for an invoice, a customer or a product, the assistant does not create it. It prepares a draft and shows it back to you — the customer, the line items, the quantities, the prices, the total. Nothing has been sent anywhere at that point. If you do nothing, nothing happens.

Confirming in the app

You tap the confirm button on the draft card. It is a deliberate tap on a specific draft, so there is no ambiguity about what you agreed to.

Confirming by voice

You have to say a confirming word — "confirm", or something that contains it. A bare "yes" is not enough, on purpose. "Yes" is the easiest thing to say by accident, and in a voice conversation it can be an answer to something else entirely. Creating an invoice in someone's books is the least undoable thing the assistant does, so it asks for a word you would only say if you meant it.

A draft expires after 30 minutes. If you leave it and come back later, it has lapsed and nothing was created. Ask again and you will get a fresh draft.

You are responsible for what you confirm. Read the draft before you confirm it — the figures in it came from what you said, and the assistant can mishear or misunderstand you.

4. What it cannot do

One thing owners ask for often enough that it belongs in writing.

It cannot send payment reminders

Tally Voice cannot chase your customers for you. It can tell you who owes you money and how overdue they are, but it cannot send a reminder, and it has no way to.

The reason is in QuickBooks Online rather than in us: its API has no operation for sending a payment reminder. Re-sending the invoice is the nearest thing available, and re-sending an invoice is not a reminder — it is the same document arriving twice, which reads to a customer as an error rather than a nudge.

If you want automatic reminders, QuickBooks has its own reminder settings and they work well. Set them up there.

5. It can be wrong, and it is not your accountant

Tally Voice reads live figures from your books, so what it tells you is grounded in real data rather than guesswork. That is the honest case for trusting it. Here is the honest case against relying on it.

It is software, and it can be wrong. It can misread a question, pull a figure from the wrong period, mishear a word in a noisy room, or give a confident answer built on a number that was miscoded in QuickBooks months ago. It has no way of knowing that something in your books is wrong; it can only read what is there.

Tally Voice is not an accountant, a bookkeeper or a registered tax agent, and nothing it says is financial, tax or legal advice. It does not know your circumstances, it is not qualified to advise you, and it is not a substitute for someone who is. If a decision matters, check it with your accountant.

What that means in practice: you remain responsible for what goes into your books and for what you lodge. An invoice you confirmed is your invoice. A figure you acted on is your decision. If something the assistant produced is wrong and you confirmed it, the record in QuickBooks is yours to correct.

BAS figures in particular

When Tally Voice tells you your BAS amount, it is reading the GST and BAS liability accounts on your Balance Sheet — "GST payable" and "BAS suspense" — and telling you what is sitting in them. That is a useful indication of where you stand, and it is not a calculated BAS.

It does not check that your transactions are coded correctly, and it cannot know about anything that has not reached your books yet. It is an aid to help you see what is coming. It is not a lodgment, and it is not a substitute for preparing one. Lodge from your accounting records, with your accountant or agent if you use one.

6. Your account

Your account is yours. One account per person — do not share a login with someone else, and do not use someone else's.

Keep your password to yourself. We store it in a scrambled form and cannot read it, which means we also cannot recover it for you, and anyone who has it can see everything in your account.

If you think someone else has your password, or that your account has been used by someone who should not have it, tell us straight away at hello@tallyvoice.ai.

One practical warning, because it catches people out: if you connect Tally Voice to a Telegram account that other people can reach, then everyone who can reach that account can see your conversation and the figures in it. The privacy policy sets this out in more detail.

7. Your QuickBooks connection

You connect QuickBooks yourself, through Intuit. You log in to Intuit, and Intuit asks you whether to give Tally Voice access. We never see your QuickBooks password.

That authorisation is yours to withdraw. You can revoke our access from inside QuickBooks at any time, without asking us and without our involvement. The moment you do, Tally Voice can no longer read your books or write to them.

You need the right to connect the company you connect. If the books belong to a business you do not run, get the owner's authority before you connect them.
